The [Sony WH-1000XM6](https://www.sony.co.uk/headphones/products/wh-1000xm6) are widely regarded as some of the best wireless noise-cancelling headphones available. I bought them as an upgrade from my ageing WH-1000XM4s, primarily because I wanted better active noise cancellation (ANC).

After testing them for 24 hours at home, outside and on several bus journeys, I returned them. The main problem was the fit: the earcups did not seal evenly around my ears, making the headphones less comfortable and the ANC less effective than I expected.

This is a focused review of the fit and real-world noise cancellation. I did not own the headphones long enough to assess long-term battery performance or durability, and neither of those factors influenced my decision to return them.

## Verdict

The WH-1000XM6 may provide excellent noise cancellation when they form a good seal, but they did not fit my head well enough to deliver that performance consistently.

The soft ear cushions applied noticeably more pressure below my ears than above them. I could not find a position that felt even or properly sealed, despite repeatedly adjusting the headband, sliders and earcups. My older WH-1000XM4s were immediately more comfortable and appeared to isolate outside noise more effectively.

Nothing else about the XM6s was a major problem during my short test. The fit alone was enough to make them unsuitable for me, because improved ANC was the main reason I bought them.

I would not recommend buying the XM6s without trying them first. Their performance depends too heavily on whether the earcups fit the individual listener.

## How I tested them

I used the XM6s for approximately 24 hours in several everyday environments:

- at home;
- outside near traffic and pedestrians;
- on several bus journeys;
- in a direct comparison with my WH-1000XM4s.

For the comparison, I switched quickly between the two pairs whilst playing the same song or radio station from my phone. I also adjusted the XM6 headband and earcup position repeatedly. I was not wearing glasses or a hat, so neither was interfering with the seal.

This was a subjective real-world comparison, not a laboratory measurement. However, it reflects the conditions in which I expected to use the headphones and was enough to determine whether they were an improvement over the pair I already owned.

## Fit and comfort

The XM6 ear cushions feel soft, but on my head they compressed unevenly. Most of the pressure was concentrated at the base of my ears, with less contact towards the top. This made the headphones less comfortable and appeared to leave a gap in the seal.

I tried changing the slider length, repositioning the cups and adjusting the headband. None of those changes produced a consistently comfortable fit. Even pressing the earcups firmly against my head with my hands did not produce the level of isolation I recognised from the XM4s.

The difference was obvious when switching back to the older headphones. Despite their age and naturally worn cushions, the XM4s distributed pressure more evenly, felt more comfortable and appeared to seal better.

Other users may have a completely different experience. Head shape, ear position and cushion compression all influence how over-ear headphones fit. The XM6 design may be comfortable for other listeners; it was not comfortable for me.

## Noise-cancelling performance

The XM6 noise cancellation was working. Removing the headphones made the surrounding traffic and voices considerably clearer. However, the overall reduction in noise did not feel better than the XM4s and sometimes seemed less effective.

During the direct comparison, I could hear broadly the same external sounds through both pairs. Cars and nearby voices remained clearly audible through the XM6s. ANC is not expected to remove every sound, but I expected a noticeable improvement over headphones that were already several years old.

The uneven fit is the most likely explanation for the disappointing result. Sony's [wearing instructions](https://helpguide.sony.net/mdr/2984/v1/en/contents/TP1001861255.html) state that the earpads should fit snugly all around the ears and warn that incorrect positioning can result in a weak noise-cancelling effect. [RTINGS also notes](https://www.rtings.com/headphones/reviews/sony/wh-1000xm6) that the XM6's noise isolation can vary depending on fit and physical factors.

Comfort and noise isolation are therefore not independent qualities. If the cushions do not create a consistent seal, passive isolation is reduced and the overall noise-cancelling effect can be weaker.

## How this compares with other reviews

Before buying the XM6s, I read and watched reviews from [SoundGuys](https://www.soundguys.com/sony-wh1000xm6-review-137397/), [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/headphones/reviews/sony/wh-1000xm6), [What Hi-Fi?](https://www.whathifi.com/headphones/sony-wh-1000xm6) and several audio reviewers on YouTube. The general conclusion was that the XM6s offered excellent ANC and were among the strongest all-round wireless headphones in their category.

Some reviews mentioned the fit, shallow earcups or thin cushions, but those points generally appeared as comfort considerations rather than prominent warnings about real-world ANC performance.

After returning the headphones, I found more detailed coverage of the issue. [SoundGuys reported that the shallow earcups and protruding internal ANC microphone could cause discomfort](https://www.soundguys.com/i-cant-bear-to-wear-the-sony-wh-1000xm6-151193/). A [later test with third-party ear pads](https://www.soundguys.com/the-sony-wh-1000xm6-have-a-comfort-problem-but-theres-an-easy-fix-152225/) found that thicker, firmer cushions produced a more consistent fit for that reviewer.

The fit limitation is therefore present in the wider review coverage, but I do not think its potential effect on ANC performance receives enough emphasis. Measurements from a test fixture show what the headphones are capable of under controlled conditions. They cannot establish whether the earcups will form an effective seal on every listener.

## Should you buy the Sony WH-1000XM6?

The XM6s are worth considering if they fit you properly. The broader test data and review coverage indicate that their ANC can be excellent, and my experience does not contradict that under the right conditions.

I would try them in a shop or borrow a pair before buying. Adjust the headband and earcups carefully, then test them somewhere with traffic and nearby conversations. Do not expect complete silence; listen for whether the headphones reduce noise as effectively as you expect and whether the seal remains comfortable.

For me, the WH-1000XM6 did not improve on the fit or real-world noise cancellation of the WH-1000XM4s. I returned them within 24 hours.
